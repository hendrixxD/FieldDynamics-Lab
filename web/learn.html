Satellite vs Ground Reality

Satellites observe 45 years of regional rainfall (CHIRPS, GPM IMERG), 250 m cropland greenness (MODIS NDVI), and ~9 km root-zone wetness (SMAP). Farmers and KADA extension officers verify laboratory soil phosphorus/sulphur, plot-level Striga history, and local seed supply.

4-Step Decision Logic

  1. Candidates: 72 two-season sequences generated from 14 savanna crops.
  2. Physics Filter: A9 FAO-56 water bucket & A15 suitability drop ill-fitting crops.
  3. 7 Dimensions: Nutrients, organic matter, erosion, pests, water, forage, market.
  4. Ranking: LGA tertile need weights multiplied 3× by farmer priorities.

Section 1 · Requirement R24

1. Satellite Observations vs Ground Measurements

Remote sensing provides consistent multi-decade coverage across all 23 Kaduna Local Government Areas, while field measurements capture plot-level chemistry and biological history invisible from orbit.

What Satellites Observe Well

  • 45-year wide-area rainfall (1981–2025): Daily onset, cessation, and seasonal totals across every LGA via CHIRPS v2.0 (0.05°, ~5.5 km) and storm intensity via GPM IMERG V07 (0.1°).
  • 250 m cropland greenness (2000–2025): 16-day canopy cover and dry-season bare-ground exposure via MODIS MOD13Q1 masked to 10 m cropland.
  • ~9 km root-zone wetness (2015–2025): Standardized root-zone soil moisture anomalies (0–100 cm) from NASA SMAP SPL4SMGP V008.
  • 30 m slope & digital soil baselines: Topographic slope (SRTM GL1) and baseline texture, pH, carbon, N, P, and S (iSDAsoil 30 m).

What Requires Ground Verification

  • Laboratory P & S soil tests: Legumes require extractable phosphorus and sulphur to nodulate effectively in Zaria and Guinea savanna Alfisols (Franke et al., 2008).
  • Village rain gauges & farmer memory: Satellite LGA averages smooth out convective village dry spells where rainfall misses single communities.
  • Field-specific Striga seedbanks: Satellites cannot detect underground witchweed seed load or past manure and fertilizer applications on a specific ridge.
  • Local seed & inoculant supply: Variety maturity class, rhizobium inoculants, and labor/traction availability require KADA officer confirmation.

Section 2 · Requirements R16, R29, R31, R43

2. Harmonized LGA × Month Grid & 4-Step Decision Engine

Data decides; published savanna agronomy checks the decision. Every sensor is harmonized onto a common cropland-masked LGA × Month and LGA × Year grid in EPSG:4326 (area and slope maths in UTM Zone 32N).

Cropland Masking & Resampling (R29, R43): All vegetation, terrain, and soil layers are masked to ESA WorldCover v200 10 m cropland (class 40) before server-side aggregation in Google Earth Engine, preventing forest reserves and urban roofs from distorting farm indicators. Continuous fields use bilinear/area-mean reduction; categorical layers preserve class rules.

Step 1 · 72 Data-Generated Sequences

Generates every two-season sequence of two distinct crops from our 14-crop savanna catalog (72 primary water-balanced two-season candidates across maize, sorghum, millet, soybean, cowpea, groundnut, sesame, cassava, tomato, plus ECOCROP season-checked crops). Published trials never whitelist or remove candidates.

Step 2 · A9 Water & A15 Suitability Filter

Under the chosen season scenario, rainfed crops running short of water (>10% ET deficit) in >50% of seasons or fitting the A2 rainy window (onset to cessation) in ≤50% of years are dropped with a logged reason. Crops fitting 50–80% of years stay with an explicit harvest-timing risk line.

Step 3 · 7 Scored Dimensions (0 to 1)

Scores Nutrients (N), Organic Matter (0.7×cover + 0.3×legume), Erosion & Infiltration (0.5×cover + 0.5×root depth), Pest/Striga Cycle, Water Use (50% chosen scenario + 50% LGA A5 climate mix), Forage, and KDBS Market Fit. Missing facts remain unscored and lower coverage weight.

Step 4 · LGA Need × 3× Priority Multipliers

Base weights (0.5, 1.0, 1.5) reflect whether the LGA sits in the best, middle, or worst third of Kaduna on iSDA N, organic C, A10 erosion, A2 dry spells, and Striga soil risk. Each farmer priority multiplies its row by 3×; high field Striga adds +2.0 to Pests. Option B must use a distinct crop set from Option A.

Independent Study Benchmark (Notebook a13_engine_validation.ipynb): Across all 23 LGAs, 4 season types, and 2 baseline monocultures (continuous maize & continuous sorghum), the 8 published Nigerian and West African Guinea savanna trial sequences rank at the 83rd percentile on average versus 47% for random sequences (10,000-draw permutation test, p = 0.0001). The 6 cereal–grain-legume rotations (sorghum→cowpea 100th, maize→cowpea 96th, sorghum→groundnut 94th, millet→groundnut 92nd, maize→groundnut 91st, maize→soybean 85th) sit at the top of the data-only ranking.

Section 5 · Requirement R30

5. How Different Functional Crop Groups Build Soil Health

Rotating crops across botanical families repairs multiple soil and pest constraints simultaneously instead of relying on synthetic nitrogen alone.

Biological Nitrogen & Striga Break

Nitrogen-Fixing Legumes

Soybean, cowpea, and groundnut host root-nodule Rhizobium bacteria that convert atmospheric N₂ into plant-available nitrogen, adding +0.38 to +1.01 t/ha to the following maize crop (Kermah et al., 2018) and stimulating suicidal germination of Striga hermonthica witchweed seeds (35% cut after 1 year, 76% after 2 years — Kureh et al., 2003). Zaria trials show legumes still need soil phosphorus (P) and sulphur (S) to fix N well (Franke et al., 2008).

Topsoil Shield & Organic Carbon

Cover & Residue-Building Crops

Leafy legumes, sorghum stover, and longer-canopy rotations narrow Kaduna's Nov–Apr bare-field window (A4), shielding exposed ridges from intense July–September monsoon raindrops (IMERG p95) and returning organic carbon that improves moisture retention during mid-season dry pauses (Ebireri, 2013).

Subsoil Biopores & Anchoring

Deep-Rooting Crops

Sorghum (2.0 m), pearl millet (2.0 m), and maize (1.7 m) rainfed root systems (FAO-56 Table 22) open vertical root channels through Hydrologic Soil Group B loams, slowing surface sheet runoff on sloping fields (SRTM 30 m) and scavenging leached subsoil moisture and nutrients.

Escape Terminal Drought

Short-Season & Drought-Tolerant Crops

Early soybean (85 d), cowpea (100 d), millet (105 d), and sesame (110 d) finish grain fill safely before October cessation when rains start 4–5 weeks late. In A9 simulations, cowpea and soybean fit ≥84% of seasons in every LGA even under Late-start scenarios where 125-day maize fits only 56%.

Section 6 · Requirements R11, R21, R22

6. Uncertainty Scoring, Spatial Limits & Human Extension Role

Every recommendation carries an explicit A12 Confidence Score (0–8 points) and standing data-gap disclosures so uncertainty is never hidden behind a polished score.

High Confidence (≥ 6 / 8)

4 LGAs: Ikara, Kubau, Lere, Makarfi. High cropland density (>74%) and cleaner skies yield crisp satellite crop masks.

Medium Confidence (4–5 / 8)

14 LGAs (including Zaria, Giwa, Sabon Gari, Soba, Birnin Gwari, Kauru, Kaura, Sanga, Jema'a, Kagarko, Kajuru, Igabi, Kudan, Zangon Kataf). Moderate monsoon cloud gap-filling or savanna tree mosaic.

Low Confidence (< 4 / 8 · Check First)

5 LGAs: Chikun, Jaba, Kachia, Kaduna North, Kaduna South. Wooded southern cloud cover or urban low-cropland share (<15%) triggers check_first = true (R22).

  • How A12 is computed: Sums four 0–2 scores: (1) ESA WorldCover cropland share, (2) cloud-filled MODIS NDVI composite fraction, (3) iSDAsoil relative uncertainty width (2/2 statewide), and (4) A6 vegetation-stress skill (0/2 everywhere until satellite stress prediction beats climatology). Confidence drops one step further if water filters must be relaxed or if soil texture is marked "Not sure".
  • Why satellites cannot separate mixed smallholder plots: A 250 m MODIS pixel spans ~6.25 hectares — blending several family farms, maize–cowpea intercrop ridges, boundary trees, and footpaths. Satellites cannot distinguish maize from soybean on a 0.5 ha field or detect localized runoff (A9 sets runoff to zero and extends iSDA subsoil below 50 cm).
  • Advisory boundary: This tool supports farmer judgment and local expertise; it never replaces them. KADA extension officers validate seed varieties, planting dates after actual village rain, and P/S fertilizer plans.
